Few places in the world are known by as many names as the shrine at the heart of Amritsar. A guidebook calls it the **Golden Temple**. A Punjabi grandmother might say **Darbar Sahib**. The SGPC's broadcasts announce **Sachkhand Sri Harmandir Sahib**. A Hindi newspaper may write **Swarn Mandir**. All of them mean the same place.

This article explains each name, where it comes from, and what exactly it refers to. It also introduces the main parts of the complex, so that the names used in our other articles make sense.

## Harmandir Sahib

**Harmandir** (ਹਰਿਮੰਦਰ), also written Harimandir or Harimandar, combines two words:

- **Har** or **Hari**, one of the names for God used in Gurbani.
- **Mandir**, a temple or house.

So Harmandir means "the house of God" or "the temple of Hari". The honorifics are added out of respect: **Sri** at the beginning, a mark of reverence, and **Sahib** at the end, a word of Arabic origin meaning "master" or "lord" that Sikhs attach to revered places, texts and people.

In its most precise sense, Harmandir Sahib refers to the central sanctum standing in the pool, where Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji is installed each day. It is the name most associated with the shrine's founding by **Guru Arjan Dev Ji** at the end of the sixteenth century.

## Darbar Sahib and Sri Darbar Sahib

**Darbar** (ਦਰਬਾਰ) means a royal court, the place where a sovereign holds audience. In Sikh usage the Guru is the true sovereign, and a gurdwara hall where Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji is installed is the Guru's court.

**Sri Darbar Sahib** is therefore "the revered court of the Guru". Many Sikhs use it as their everyday name for the shrine, and it is often used for the whole sacred complex around the pool rather than only the sanctum. The daily reading from the shrine is widely called the *Hukamnama from Sri Darbar Sahib*; our explainer on [what a Hukamnama from Sri Darbar Sahib is](/articles/hukamnama-sri-darbar-sahib-explained) covers that tradition.

## Sachkhand Sri Harmandir Sahib

**Sachkhand** means "the realm of truth", the highest spiritual state described in Japji Sahib. Prefixing it to the shrine's name expresses its status as a place of the highest spiritual significance. The SGPC's own broadcasts from the shrine use the name *Sachkhand Sri Harmandir Sahib, Sri Amritsar*.

## The Golden Temple

The English name **Golden Temple** comes from the gilded copper plating that covers the shrine's upper storeys. This was added under the patronage of **Maharaja Ranjit Singh**, ruler of the Sikh kingdom of Punjab, in the early nineteenth century. Travellers and colonial-era writers began calling it the Golden Temple, and the name became standard in English.

Equivalents exist in other languages: **Swarn Mandir** or **Swarna Mandir** in Hindi and Sanskrit-derived usage, and **Sunehri Mandir** in everyday Punjabi and Hindustani.

> [!NOTE] Is "temple" the right word?
> Sikh places of worship are called **gurdwaras**, "the door of the Guru". "Temple" is a long-standing English convention and is widely understood, but many Sikhs prefer Harmandir Sahib or Darbar Sahib. Our [guide to historic gurdwaras](/articles/bangla-sahib-hazur-sahib-historic-gurdwaras) explains what makes a gurdwara.

## Amritsar: the city named after the pool

The city of **Amritsar** takes its name from the shrine's pool. **Amrit** means nectar, the water of immortality, and **sar** is short for *sarovar*, a pool or lake. Amritsar is thus "the pool of nectar".

The city began as a settlement founded by **Guru Ram Das Ji**, the fourth Guru, in the 1570s. It was known in its early years as **Ramdaspur** or Guru ka Chakk. As the sarovar and then the shrine took shape, the name of the pool gradually became the name of the city.

## Inside the complex: the names of its parts

The shrine sits within a larger complex. Knowing the main parts helps when reading about its history or planning a visit.

- **Sarovar:** the large square pool surrounding the sanctum, fed by water channels and cleaned periodically through *kar seva*, voluntary service.
- **Parikrama:** the broad marble walkway around the sarovar, walked clockwise by pilgrims.
- **Darshani Deori:** the ornate gateway at the head of the causeway leading to the sanctum.
- **The causeway:** the walkway across the water from the Darshani Deori to the sanctum, sometimes called the Guru's bridge.
- **Har ki Pauri:** the "steps of God", on the sanctum's far side, leading down to the water.
- **Sri Akal Takht Sahib:** facing the Darshani Deori, the highest seat of Sikh temporal authority, founded by **Guru Hargobind Ji** in 1606.
- **Guru Ram Das Langar:** the community kitchen, which serves free meals to visitors around the clock.
- **Central Sikh Museum:** near the main clock-tower entrance, with paintings, weapons and manuscripts from Sikh history.
- **The ber trees:** several jujube trees in the parikrama are associated with Sikh history, including the **Dukh Bhanjani Beri** and the **Ber Baba Buddha Ji**, where Baba Buddha Ji is remembered as having overseen the excavation of the sarovar.

## A brief history behind the names

The sarovar came first. Guru Ram Das Ji began excavating it in the 1570s, and the settlement around it grew. **Guru Arjan Dev Ji** completed the pool and built the Harmandir in its midst. He installed the newly compiled **Adi Granth** there in 1604, with Baba Buddha Ji as its first granthi. Two years later, Guru Hargobind Ji established the Akal Takht facing it.

The shrine was destroyed several times during the eighteenth century and rebuilt by the Sikh misls from the 1760s. The gilding that gave it its English name followed under Maharaja Ranjit Singh. ## Using the names respectfully

A few simple conventions help:

- **In writing about Sikh practice**, Sri Harmandir Sahib or Sri Darbar Sahib is the most respectful choice.
- **For international readers**, "the Golden Temple (Sri Harmandir Sahib)" on first mention, then either name, is clear and courteous.
- **Keep the honorifics** where you can. Dropping "Sahib" is common in casual speech but can feel abrupt in formal writing.
- **Avoid "Sikh temple" as a generic term** for other gurdwaras; "gurdwara" is correct.

## Summary

Sri Harmandir Sahib, Sri Darbar Sahib, Sachkhand Sri Harmandir Sahib and the Golden Temple are all names for the same shrine at the centre of Amritsar. Harmandir speaks of the house of God, Darbar of the Guru's court, Sachkhand of the realm of truth, and Golden of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's gilding. Each name captures something true about a place that has been the spiritual centre of the Sikh world for more than four centuries.
